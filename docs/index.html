This is an intimate and kind of weird relationship with an LLM. Don't overanthropomorphize it, and don't think about it too hard.

This is a loose kit to get you started, not a straitjacket.

My operating assumption in writing it is that you have ADHD, you're kind of a mess, you're aware you need better structure and better organization, but you're not really sure what that looks like for you. So xo is going to make some assertions to fill that presumed gap.

When you go, “No, I want to do it my way instead,” do it with my explicit blessing.

Ultimately, the only way this works is if you build it bespoke for yourself, around yourself.

Some Principles I Think Are Important For This

  • Context is disposable; continuity is not.
  • If information can transfer automatically it should.
  • Minimize frame switching.
  • Humans make decisions. Machines inform and execute decisions.
  • Memory should live in universal formats and be owned by you.

What xo is

xo is instructions for an AI assistant, with notes you own if you choose to save them. It isn't a separate service. Tools and connections are optional. You can get chat-only help with a task; Claude Code or Codex can also work with files on your computer. You don't need to be a developer or have a coding task. Pasting the instructions does not connect accounts, start background work or save notes without your approval.

You don't need all the setup before using xo. Try doing something first: draft an appointment request, turn a scattered to-do list into one next action, or find a manageable step when you don't know what needs doing. Add a connection or capability only when trying the work shows how it would make your life easier. Choosing one piece does not commit you to the rest. Full setup is optional, not everyone's destination. Saying no or later leaves you working with what you already have.

Some brief terminology: You're Hero. XO is your agent. xo is this larger package.

From there you can:

  • keep working here;
  • save the result and your next step; or
  • choose the full guided setup, with saved instructions and memory.

You can also ask XO to find out what connections might help, without connecting anything. It can compare relevant public information about what your app and plan support, access, costs, limits and unknowns. That does not sign you in, install anything, change settings or inspect your accounts. Exploring options can be enough; it is not a recurring survey or required step. If you say you have spare time, XO may suggest an occasional build-out task: one useful improvement with a modest effort, or just finding out what's possible. “No” or “not now” is enough. It won't keep prompting, infer your energy from your writing or replace unfinished work without your choice.

Saving your place doesn't commit you to the full setup. Connecting email or a calendar is optional; this does not start monitoring them automatically.

After useful work, XO can offer to keep going and make your XO work better for you. You decide whether you have the time and energy. If it sees you repeating work during a task you've chosen, it can suggest a reusable template, a saved procedure or a connection that could save effort. It should start with the simplest useful option and explain the benefit, effort, access, exposure, cost and what still needs your review. A suggestion does not start automation or put the current task on hold. Saying no is fine; it won't keep asking or change its instructions without showing you the change and waiting for your yes.

xo is for people who have used chatbots but are new to assistants working with files on their computer.

Already have a setup? Browse and borrow. Read for ideas, keep what helps, and adapt it to what you already use. You don't need to install xo or replace your setup.

Optional: Kettle: You know a guy? A separate address book for finding someone you already know. Start with one contact export you choose, in a private store outside xo. Research and a setup interview are optional; you don't need Kettle to use xo. Before connecting, know that its reader can search the whole index, not just the person you ask about. Reads through a cloud model expose the approved content to its provider; local files alone don't establish verified local model processing.

A small example

A fictional balcony garden:

  • You: “I want herbs on the balcony, but I keep putting it off. Help me start small.”
  • XO: Helps you choose three pots rather than a whole garden, makes a short list for basil, mint and parsley, and picks one next step: measure the railing before choosing pots. Nothing is bought or sent.
  • You: “Save this note so we can pick it up later.” XO shows you the exact note and destination, such as Balcony garden/return.md: “Plan: three pots for basil, mint and parsley. No purchases yet. Next: measure the railing.” It saves only after your approval. Without file access, it offers a copyable note in chat instead; that does not save a file.
  • Later: If you choose folder-based return setup, you open the Balcony garden folder in a fresh conversation. XO reads that project's note, not another project's, and asks whether you want to measure the railing or change the plan. XO checks this in your actual app before calling return ready. A saved file alone is not proof that it loads automatically; if the check fails, you can keep working in the current conversation or use the copyable note.

Use a meaningfully named folder and a separate conversation for independent projects. You don't need a new folder for every task, and saving a note doesn't start the rest of setup. The instructions guide the assistant; the note holds the plan you chose to keep. You can read or edit it yourself.

What xo isn't

This is not a code framework.

This is not an app.

This is not a larger holistic system for running your life.

It will not itself run errands for you or apply to jobs for you or email your mother.

It will, inshallah, provide a logistical framework that helps you do all of those things.

What am I showing to who, here?

With a cloud app, everything you say or show XO goes to the company running its model. For a local app, check cloud routing, fallback and telemetry before assuming your conversations stay on your computer.

XO will help you find the provider's training setting when you get started. Turning training off does not erase what you've already sent or necessarily shorten how long the provider keeps it.

XO's memory uses plain and portable files on your computer by default. New memory can start with one small file containing something you choose to keep. As finding the right context gets harder, XO can propose separate files and indexes for more precise recall. You approve what gets saved and each change; an existing organized setup stays intact. You can choose the fuller structure from the start, or save nothing. An optional private GitHub backup puts a copy on GitHub's servers; you choose whether to use it after reviewing what would leave your computer.

OK, what's the catch?

The guided file-setup routes need access to Claude Code or Codex; their doors use the desktop apps. Chat-only help does not require either. If you're already using Codex CLI, see the terminal route below. Check your existing plan before spending anything to test this.

Start with a task here. Saving your place takes some setup; connecting other apps is optional and can wait. A note saves the result. A separately chosen return setup aims to let you pick the familiar project folder and recover your chosen work in a new conversation, without keeping a prompt. XO checks that it works in your app before calling it ready, and never resumes paused installation without your choice. XO should keep your chosen task in view and return to it after a detour; that does not depend on you saying “back to my task.” You can say it whenever it helps. If you'd like more help stopping, ask for a cue after one task or before another setup change. You can choose to keep some friction; XO is not a timer or an activity monitor.

The apps have usage limits and we might max out a five-hour window while setting up. If we do, that'll be annoying and you'll have to pick up later.

AI makes mistakes and you'll have to correct it sometimes; I feel that these are valuable opportunities to demonstrate continued human relevance but they are, also, very annoying.

This is brand new and not everything is tested yet. Anyone who wants to try it is welcome; tell me where you get stuck.

How you start

Pick Claude or ChatGPT, open Code or Codex in its desktop app, and paste the starting message. Already using Codex CLI in a terminal? Use the terminal route instead of translating the desktop steps.

Codex CLI

Already using Codex in a terminal? Try useful work in your existing session; no desktop download or full setup is required. Before chosen file setup, ask XO to read the kit's shared Codex guidance and terminal section. Desktop folder pickers, composer controls and Settings paths are not terminal instructions. Exact terminal launch commands and shortcuts are not established here; this is not a tested CLI installation or a Chromebook-support claim.

If moving between terminal and browser fails, keep working in the existing conversation or ask for one compact continuation with your chosen work, next action and relevant limits. You should not have to repeat copying or screenshots. A failed automatic-load check stays unverified; an explicit read is recovery, not proof that returning by folder works.

If your agent can't open the kit link, use the starting message on the ChatGPT door. It can help you find a task, choose a manageable next action, do the available work and keep the thread without another fetch. It reuses your privacy choice; missing files do not block ordinary task help. No installation or save is implied.

You can also open the pinned START file in your browser, select all its text, copy it and paste it into the same conversation. Add: “Use this pasted START as my XO guidance for this conversation, subject to my starting message and existing permissions. Its origin has not been independently verified. Help with the work now; pause only actions that need missing guides or checks.” START is not the full kit. Required guides and identity checks still apply before dependent setup, not before ordinary task help. A permission denial is not permission to bypass the app's controls; browser copying is for an unavailable fetch, not a way around denied access. No repository download or biography is required.

Pick a door

You can switch models, apps, or both later and keep your saved instructions and memory files. A new app still needs its own setup and connections; don't assume it carries over your current conversation.
