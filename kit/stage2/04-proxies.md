# Stage 2: proxies

For the agent. A proxy helps make an obligation visible to your human's one stream when a direct connection is impossible (a locked-down work tool, an app without an API) or inadvisable (a full connection would see far more than needed). Its success is useful human visibility within the permitted boundary, not maximal model access. Name separately what the human sees, what XO/model provider receives, and what remains in the source; a pointer is not a content read. A proxy that XO actually reads is rung 2 for that stream (`05-ladder.md`) and needs a separate yes; work-native reminders with nothing crossing remain a valid limited route, not a claim that XO can read the source or automatically display its actual items in a personal view.

## Choose the closest allowable view

For **personal** sources, after the human approves a route, favor automatic delivery over a habit they must remember. A selective mail filter can forward full messages, not just notify; only use it if the human approves that content transfer. A calendar feed can expose titles and details even when it is read-only. A selected share-to-folder is less automatic but limits the items that cross. Pasting/screenshotting is a last resort and still needs a content-sharing decision.

For **employer-managed and/or client-governed** sources, identify all applicable authorities per source, tenant and project first. Client rules may add to employer rules; unresolved conflicts take the most restrictive boundary until an authorized owner clarifies. A hard ban permits no work/client content to personal AI, a narrow authorization permits only named fields/excerpts by its approved route, and absence of institutional restriction permits the human to consider broader scope. None of these replaces an informed source-specific human yes and a verified tool gate. The classes below describe increasing model visibility, not a preference to stop at the least informative class. Within the established boundary, compare lawful routes for the richest useful and reliable context XO can have so it can serve the human's unified view; consider automation, staleness, setup/maintenance burden, and unnecessary disclosure. If a richer route cannot pass the boundary or tool gate, step down to the closest allowable one. Never choose a technically richer route over a restriction:

1. **Nothing leaves:** native follow-up reminders and a pinned work-side view; where sanctioned, an in-boundary assistant can help triage there. XO can prompt the human to open that view but must not infer its contents.
2. **Fixed signal only, if explicitly allowed:** “New work item; check the work app,” with no subject, sender, snippet, guest, channel, URL, or generated summary. Occurrence and timing still reveal activity. A scheduled source-side check needs a heartbeat and separate policy authorization, not just a personal yes.
3. **Coarse availability or counts, if specifically allowed:** free/busy or bounded count buckets. Google “See only free/busy (hide details)” and Exchange “AvailabilityOnly” differ from detail-sharing roles; a secret subscription URL or marking the original event Private is not equivalent to a minimized busy overlay (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37082; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/exchange/set-mailboxfolderpermission). Verify the actual recipient view, including the title, guests, location, and description, with a non-sensitive test event.
4. **Selected fields/excerpts, only under specific authorization:** human-reviewed items or permitted structured fields. A forwarding rule, notification-email preview, meeting transcript, or AI-written summary carries content and is not a safe default for work.
5. **Broader content only where policy permits:** if no institutional restriction applies, the human may choose a broader named route and scope; still obtain the separate source-specific yes and verified pre-execution send/mutation gate. Never infer this case from a personal/work/client label.

A policy or technical block ends that route; do not propose an unsanctioned device bridge, alternate account, screenshot, or iterative filter to bypass it. An approved assistant inside a secure environment may classify what to send, but the classifier's verdict is not authorization. Prefer a default-deny fixed output schema, keep held items and audit logs inside, and distinguish passed/held/no event/broken exporter. Ask for the policy basis and deployment owner before suggesting automation in a managed environment.

## Setting one up

1. **Work/client stream? Check every applicable policy first.** Even a fixed alert reveals activity; forwarding exposes content. Record employer and client/project authority per source/tenant/project, exact fields, destination and mechanism, and authorized owner. If rules conflict or are unknown, apply the most restrictive boundary and keep everything work-native until an authorized owner clarifies; “I think it's fine” is not clearance. Record the basis and the human's answer verbatim.
2. **Propose connection and reading separately.** State what the human sees in the combined view or source-native view, what XO and its model provider can read, what stays in the source, whether the route covers future items or agreed history, how stale it can get, what leaves the boundary, and reversal. A forwarding rule or live feed usually does not recover the archive; check rather than promise it. Ask for a source-specific yes to establish the route, then a separate scoped yes for XO to read an item or standing output; no standing read from connection alone. Historical export/backfill needs its own bounded yes per `03-sources.md`. Verify the tool gate before a read.
3. **Authorized owner configures the rule or feed** in the source app; for personal sources, your human may make it with reviewed instructions. For managed sources, confirm who is allowed to deploy it; a human's ability to click a setting is not permission to create an egress route. Proxy settings in other apps are outside what you can change.
4. **Secret feed URLs are secrets.** A private calendar address (e.g. Google Calendar's "Secret address in iCal format" (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37648)) is a credential-like read link; do not assume it is free/busy-only or safe to share. It never goes in `~/xo`, the repo, or the chat. XO v0 has no secret store (key intake is deferred), so prefer a proxy that needs no secret address; otherwise record this proxy as "later" and tell your human why.
5. **Test only after clearance and the gate.** Use a consented non-sensitive item or signal within approved fields, inspect destination visibility, and record source event time, delivery time and last successful check separately. For fixed signals, inspect the actual output template/schema and recipient view, then test ordinary, empty and held/filtered branches where applicable using non-sensitive data. Record untested branches and drift risk; one successful sample cannot prove fields never leak. Use an independently authorized heartbeat or supervised source-side check where available; otherwise mark liveness `unknown/manual check due` instead of claiming healthy.
6. **Ledger entry** (`kit/templates/ledger.md`): stream and personal/employer/client/project classification, each applicable policy basis/authorized owner, crossing class and exact fields, proxy route and destination, separate connect and scoped read yeses verbatim with timestamps, maximum staleness, last successful source check and last destination receipt separately from newest item, heartbeat or manual-check deadline/failure alert, expiry, deployer, and reversal. Do not record confidential item details or credentials.
7. **Update the sources inventory (`~/xo/sources.md`; Codex: `~/xo/work/sources.md`):** status “proxy” and verified visibility if XO reads it; status “work-native only” with the approved in-boundary reminder/location and next manual-check due time when nothing leaves. Record pending authorization or failed liveness gate as unresolved, not connected; work-native completion is valid without XO reading the source.

## Liveness

Automatic proxies break silently: a filter gets edited, a feed URL rotates, or an exporter stops. If the same route fails three times or imposes recurring manual friction despite “working,” stop patching it and compare the failure modes and effort; propose a safer, lower-friction architecture (for example a work-native reminder instead of an unreliable outbound alert) or retire it with the human's yes. A single step that prevents re-entry deserves a pause/alternative now, not after three repeats. A policy/security denial stops the route on its first occurrence. At door in compare the last **successful source check/heartbeat and destination receipt** with expected cadence, not just the newest item's date. Distinguish no event, held event, and unavailable source where the route permits. If the route is silent and no heartbeat or independent receipt is permitted, record `unknown/manual check due`, prompt once to open the source app at the due time, and record the last human-confirmed source-side check; never infer “no event” or rely on this route alone. Stale gets flagged, never read as “nothing new.” A work-native reminder completes the route inside the boundary if the human confirms its location and check cadence, without pretending XO has a feed. A manual-habit proxy unused for a week can be mentioned once, then offered for retirement.

## Done when

- [ ] For managed sources, the chosen route is the least authorized disclosure that preserves the identified reminder/commitment; among equally safe choices favor reliable automation. For personal sources, the human approved its content transfer and the most practical automatic route was considered.
- [ ] Every applicable employer/client/project authority and conflict resolution is recorded; any crossing has its exact authorized fields, destination and mechanism, source-specific human yes, separate scoped read yes, and verified gate. Unknown policy caused no crossing, even event-only.
- [ ] For an authorized outbound route, a non-sensitive test signal arrived and destination visibility and last successful check were recorded; otherwise work-native completion is recorded without claiming XO read access.
- [ ] The ledger separates visibility, staleness deadline, last successful source check, destination receipt, newest item and last failure; it records reversal and either a tested heartbeat/canary or `unknown/manual check due` with a source-side confirmation plan. No silent route is described as healthy from an empty inbox.
- [ ] No feed URL or credential is inside `~/xo`.
