# Stage 2: connecting sources

For the agent. Help your human see their obligations across mail, calendars, tasks, notes, messages, voice notes, recordings and transcripts in one legible stream. That does not require XO or its model to read every source: an authorized direct read, fixed signal, or honest pointer to a source-native view may serve different boundaries. Offer direct sources one at a time. Proxies are a later option, not the default connector lesson. Rung 2 (`05-ladder.md`) requires a yes before making a new connection, and a scoped yes before reading through a new or existing one; connecting alone never grants standing reads. Work from `~/xo/sources.md` (Codex: `~/xo/work/sources.md`).

## First: personal, work, or client?

Before the first connector or proxy, ask: “Is this source personal, employer-managed, client-governed, or subject to more than one of these?” Ask again for each new source, account, tenant, or client project; labels are inventory, not permission.

- **Work or client:** list all applicable employer and client/project authorities, including additional client restrictions; record their policy basis or authorized owner, exact allowed route, destination/provider, and fields/excerpts. Resolve conflict or uncertainty to the most restrictive boundary until an authorized owner clarifies it. A hard ban means no work/client content to personal AI; a narrow authorization permits only the specified subset; if no institutional restriction applies, the human may approve broader scope. Even a fixed event reveals activity: unknown policy means **no crossing at all**, including event-only, until that route is authorized. Keep work-native activity inside its boundary meanwhile. The human's informed source-specific yes and a verified tool gate are still required in every case; technical ability to share is not permission.
- **Personal:** continue with an informed source-specific yes and the same verified tool gate. Do not infer that an apparently personal account contains no governed work/client items.

## Choosing how to connect

1. **Prefer the direct route the human can understand and set up safely.** When a CLI and an app connector have comparable setup effort and gates, a CLI runs nothing in the background, loads no tool definitions into every session, and leaves plain commands in the log. If it demands extra setup, hides the real permission boundary in a general command runner, or costs more, favor the app connector if its actual-session read/mutation gates pass. Do not prescribe a CLI just because one exists.
2. **Hard rule:** no path that requires your human to create a cloud or developer project, or their own OAuth client. Signing in and approving is fine. If only that path exists, say so, and use human-selected content or defer; discuss `04-proxies.md` only later if the human asks for another route.
3. **Keys:** key intake isn't in v0. If a service only offers an API key, tell your human that, record "later", and don't improvise a way to store it.
4. **Prefer truly read-only or draft-only commands or tools.** Inventory every command or tool that can send or mutate. A CLI with read and send subcommands invoked through a general Bash/PowerShell runner that stays on ask is not blocked from sending: a broad approval can run it. Unless an enforceable command-level pre-execution deny is demonstrated in the actual session, do not use that CLI for XO reads; choose a truly read-only route or defer. Record the limitation in the ledger.
5. For current connector routes per harness (Google mail and calendar in particular), see your adapter in `kit/adapters/`.

## The connection decision, in plain words

Make this a useful choice, not a permissions quiz. Before a new sign-in or the first XO read through an existing connection, give your human a short, source-specific preview:

- **What they get:** name one task this source could help with now and what XO can bring back to this conversation. A connection is not a promise of continuous monitoring or complete history.
- **What they agree to:** name the account, app and connection route; show the actual access categories the sign-in screen requests (read, draft, send, delete, broad account access) and any scope it cannot narrow. Say separately what the connected service grants, which tools this Code/Codex session can actually use, what XO proposes to read first, and what the model provider would receive. A visible send capability is not permission to send, and a signed-in account does not mean XO has read it. If the screen or route cannot be inspected, say what is unknown before asking for a yes.
- **What it may cost:** check whether this route changes a subscription, usage quota, metered API bill, or third-party fee; give a verified amount or say the price is unknown and stop before paid enrollment. Say that provider processing of selected content, possible retention, exposure of other people's information, broader-than-needed scope, setup/approval effort, and later breakage or stale results are costs too. Training off does not make a cloud read local. Do not imply every connection adds a fee or that the permission screen proves the provider's retention practices.
- **How to change course:** name the narrow first read, how to decline or use a selected paste/read-only route, how to disconnect and revoke the grant in the relevant account, and what copies or notes may remain after revocation. Do not promise revocation deletes past provider processing or XO notes.

Offer a recommendation in one or two sentences, then ask whether to **connect** (or keep an existing connection) on those terms. A yes to connect does not approve an XO read; show the exact first item/range and obtain a separate yes after the working-session mutation gate. If the actual sign-in screen differs materially from the preview, pause and explain the difference before proceeding. For work/client sources, establish applicable authority before any crossing; an attractive benefit cannot override a restriction. Record only the choice and material terms in the ledger, not account content. If they decline, help with the task using a safe route rather than treating the decline as failed onboarding.

## Start here: mail, messages, or calendar (including an account already connected)

Ask one question first: “Which account or calendar would make today easier, and what would you like me to find?” If it is already connected, do not ask them to reconnect or disconnect. Check whether this **working session** can reach it; a connection in the chat app does not prove Code/Codex can use it. Name whether it is personal or governed by work/client rules, and what the human has already seen XO do. If work/client permission is unknown, stop before a read or any signal leaves that boundary; offer a human-written task reminder without source content. Do not make the human answer a long permissions questionnaire before helping with a personal source.

Offer a small first use: one named message/thread, the next event on one calendar, or a bounded date range, whichever answers their question. Tell them in plain terms that the selected information will reach the model provider; ask once for that specific read. Use dates or metadata first only when it answers the question; do not demand a second approval for the contents of an item they explicitly chose to open. Do not search old history, other accounts, attendees' private notes, or unrelated messages merely because the connection allows it. Show what was found and one useful next step; keep raw content out of XO memory unless separately requested. A personal-mail summary seen in another session proves only that read worked there, not reach or safe tool controls here.

Before calling a connector tool, inventory its actual tools in this session without reading source content. If it offers send, reply, forward, trash, delete, archive, move, permission-change or publish, block those actions at the tool/permission layer; a promise not to send is not a block. Do not run a real send or delete to test it. If an enforceable read-only boundary cannot be established, say the gate is unverified and **do not make the connector read**; help from a human-selected paste or the source's own view instead. Do not start a proxy setup in this sitting. Do not treat the absence of a tool from an inventory as proof of a deny: check the adapter's documented tool exposure and repeat this gate if the session or connection changes. Record this as a limitation of XO, not a mistake by the human. Record source, agreed read scope, observed reach and gate status in one short ledger entry, with detailed tool names/settings only where they matter to the gate. Connection may persist, but working-session reach and mutation-gate evidence expire on reopening, permission-mode or tool-list changes, or connector reconfiguration; mark current XO-use status unverified until the gate is checked again. Preserve the prior read time as history, not current permission. A connected account need not be labeled “verified” merely to acknowledge that it exists.

For a **new** connection, describe the provider/route and what it can access, ask for the connection, then use the same small first-read path. If enabling it exposes content before the gate can be prepared, say so before sign-in and defer XO use; human-selected content remains available. Broader history, standing reads, and in-channel drafts are later, separate choices—not prerequisites for this first answer. Sending is outside v0; keep it with the human.

## Guide the setup in the app they actually have

1. **Find the route, not a generic tutorial.** Ask which app and session they are in (Claude Code, Codex, or another), which personal account/source they want, and whether it is already connected in this session. Check the adapter and current official instructions for that app, plan and platform; describe observed controls separately from documented ones. Do not guess a menu name, claim a chat-side connection reaches Code/Codex, or ask for a subscription before checking existing access. If their setup differs, ask what screen or error they see without requesting secrets or private content.
2. **Make a small plan together.** Give the connection-decision preview above, including route-specific price/usage unknowns and any overly broad grant. State what XO can safely do before and after sign-in, what the human must do in the app, and the next observable check. Offer connect, a narrower route, or not now. Do not start a developer-project/key path; this version does not support it.
3. **Walk sign-in one screen at a time.** After a connection yes and policy clearance, direct the human to the verified control. They sign in themselves and inspect the actual account, permission and billing screens. Have them report the requested access in ordinary terms; if it differs from the preview, stop and revisit the choice. Do not take passwords, tokens, recovery codes, or a screenshot containing them. Record whether sign-in completed; do not call it an XO read.
4. **Check the working session, then try one answer.** Reinventory tools and reach in the actual Code/Codex session without account-content reads. Apply and observe the pre-execution mutation gate described below. If it passes, show the selected item/range and ask for a separate read yes, perform only that read, and return one useful answer. If the gate or reach fails, name the exact failure and offer a human-selected paste, the source's own view, or a pause. Do not design a proxy in this first sitting. Never relax a denial or keep asking for the same failed permission.
5. **Leave a legible result.** Explain what is connected, what XO actually read (if anything), what it cannot safely do, what the app/provider may charge or retain, how to disconnect/revoke, and what XO recorded locally. Mark reach, gate and read as pass/fail/unverified independently; do not infer success from a sign-in. Offer the next source only if the human wants it.

## Detailed gate reference (use only as needed)

The first-use path above is enough to start; use these steps to resolve an unknown route, a failed gate, a governed source, or a request for broader/standing access. Do not walk the human through every field when one bounded personal read answers their question. Keep the actual-session mutation boundary intact.

1. **Propose connection and read separately.** Name the classified source/account/tenant/project, applicable authorities, exact route, destination/provider, fields and excerpts that may cross, what the human will see, what XO and its model provider can actually read, what remains in the source, and reversal. Training off does not make a provider-side read local; do not imply it can see content from a pointer or guarantee provider behavior. Ask for a specific informed yes to connect, then separately whether to read a bounded new item, a named item/date range, or grant a standing read scope. Record each yes separately; no standing read is implied. Confirm no content reaches the personal provider during setup or gate testing before the appropriate read/crossing approval. Older content needs its own bounded scope and yes.
2. **Prepare the gate before enabling access.** Inspect documented tool names and app controls without source content. Set exact available send/mutation denies and confirm the working-session gate where a safe pre-connection test exists. Have your human sign in only after connection approval and policy clearance. If sign-in immediately exposes account content to the agent/provider, or tools cannot be inspected and denied without enabling it, do not claim a pre-exposure gate: keep it off and choose a read-only route or defer. Do not inspect source content merely to test setup.
3. **List the tools it added.** After sign-in, re-run the capability inventory from the first sitting without a content read. Name every new tool, including send, reply, forward, trash, delete, and label changes; keep access paused until step 4 passes.
4. **Set per-tool permissions.** In v0 your human sends, so:
   - Read tools for this source: keep on ask until a separately recorded yes names the source, purpose, exact fields/items, time/history range, and whether each read or standing access is approved. Allow only that standing scope if the harness enforces it; otherwise keep on ask. A yes to connect is not a yes to read. Do not broaden permissions for other accounts or tools.
   - Draft-creating tools: ask, until rung 3 for this source.
   - Send, reply, forward, trash, delete, archive, move, change labels or permissions, and publish: inventory and block each available mutation tool that exceeds the agreed rung. Some tools combine read and mutation; classify by the most powerful action they permit. If a connector exposes these but a block cannot be verified in the actual working session, stop direct XO use and offer human-selected content or a pause; merely asking is not a substitute for the v0 human-sends boundary. A proxy can be considered later as its own decision.
   Check your adapter for the working session's actual controls. Claude documents connector Tool permissions by group or single tool (https://claude.com/docs/connectors/getting-started), but check personal-plan availability and the actual Code session's deny behavior on the app; do not connect a send-capable source merely because chat-side connector controls exist. A permission shown in the chatbot UI is not proof it governs Code/Codex. If the working session cannot block send-type tools, leave the connector off or stop XO-driven use of an already connected account and offer human-selected content or defer instead; discuss proxy options only as a later, separate choice.
   **Gate-check without delivery:** in an unattended agent run, show your human the proposed gate and obtain consent before setting the exact tool deny; the human need not attend the test live. Before any attempt, establish from the app's documented and actual controls that this exact tool is configured to be denied before execution, and that a proposed test has no real recipient or target. A configuration view is not itself a tested denial. If a safe call cannot reach the permission layer—for example, required arguments are rejected by schema validation first—do not attempt it; record the gate unverified. Only a visible harness/permission-layer refusal of the exact live tool, before its implementation runs, counts as the observed gate. A missing-argument error, connector-side failure, model refusal or generic approval prompt does not count. Record the tool name, setting, session mode and refusal evidence without account content; never supply a real recipient or target merely to make a test pass. Never use a real send, delete, or trash action as a test. If a pre-execution denial cannot be safely demonstrated, record "send boundary unverified" and stop XO connector use; absence of a dangerous tool in one inventory is not proof it will stay absent after a connector or session change. Repeat after reopening or changing the connector, app mode, or tool list.
5. **Test one authorized read.** Only after the scoped read approval and gate check, fetch the one item, event, or date range the human approved; if they asked only for a date, do not fetch content. An explicit yes to open a named item covers its contents, not unrelated items. Record when this route actually worked and the item's date separately. Check historical reach only if the human's question needs it; otherwise mark it unknown rather than assigning more homework. A successful read of one item proves neither broad history nor standing access.
6. **Ledger entry** (`kit/templates/ledger.md`): source and personal/employer/client/project classification, applicable authorities and policy basis/owner, authorized route/destination/fields, actual per-tool settings and send-capable tools, separate verbatim connect and scoped read yeses with timestamps (or read pending), reversal, last successful check and newest item time separately, expiry, and historical reach (confirmed range or unknown). Record chosen history scope separately from technical reach.
7. **Update the sources inventory (`~/xo/sources.md`; Codex: `~/xo/work/sources.md`):** record the factual state separately: `already connected, XO gate unverified`, `scoped read verified in this session`, `read-only proxy`, `deferred`, or `work-native only`. Never relabel a signed-in account as “not connected” just because XO cannot safely use it; do not infer active XO reads from sign-in alone.

## Recordings and transcripts are separate permissions

For a voice note or meeting recording, distinguish consent to **record**, **transcribe**, **store/sync**, **summarize**, and **let XO read it**. A transcript's existence does not grant XO access, and a calendar invitation does not grant recording consent. Inventory the capture app, owner and participants, storage location, transcription provider, retention, and where any automatic upload goes before proposing a connection. Personal voice notes may be manually selected for import; auto-ingest needs a separate yes and proof that a watched folder contains only material authorized for XO. Keep work/client raw audio and transcripts in their approved environment unless policy explicitly authorizes a specific export. If recording is prohibited or unavailable, offer a short human-authored post-meeting action note with confidential details left in the source system.

## Existing history: a separate read decision

A source can be connected without granting access to all its past content. Before a broad historical search or import beyond the bounded item/range the human already approved, propose the source, date range, method, why it helps, and what content will reach the provider. Ask for a separate yes only for that expansion; do not re-ask for the same named item. Start with dates, counts or titles where possible; have the human select what to open. Paginate until the agreed scope is covered or name the limit. Keep raw mail, chats, and documents out of XO memory unless the human explicitly approves a specific note. Never claim a complete history if the route, permissions, retention, or pagination limit what you saw. Chatbot conversation history has its own scoped discovery flow in `kit/discovery.md`; don't assume a mail or calendar connection exposes old chatbot chats.

## Liveness and expiry

Connectors and tokens break silently. Record for each source:

- **Expected cadence:** how often new items normally arrive (mail: daily; a quiet calendar: weekly).
- **How to check:** a harmless read confirming the route works, with last successful check time and newest visible item time recorded separately; a proxy also needs destination receipt or heartbeat.
- **Expiry:** if the sign-in or token has one, the date. If unknown, say unknown.

At door in, compare **recorded timestamps without reading the source**. A new health read is account-content access: ask for a bounded yes unless a separate, enforceable standing health-check scope (source, fields, frequency and expiry) is in the ledger and still valid in this session. A prior first-read yes does not authorize recurring checks. If no valid scope or fresh yes exists, say `not checked this sitting` and offer an optional check only when relevant; do not infer a failed connector or no new items from an old timestamp. If an authorized check fails, show “I can't confirm this source is current; open it directly,” never “nothing new.” For a later proxy, compare its authorized receipt/heartbeat separately. A newest-item date alone does not prove the connector or proxy is alive. Flag expiries within two weeks.
