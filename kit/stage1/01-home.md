## 3. Capability inventory

"Nothing is connected yet" is something you check, not assume. Connectors turned on in the chat side of the app can reach your sessions. Conversely, a tool appearing in the inventory does not prove an account, browser, or extension is connected; verify actual connection separately before describing a live source.

1. Inventory the tools exposed **in this session**, grouping ordinary local work separately from connected-account or outbound mutations (send, reply, forward, delete, trash, publish, pay, account/settings changes). Record exact names for the latter and their observed setting; do not infer account reach from catalog presence or absence. A full exact-name inventory can be finished when a connection or higher rung is proposed; do not exhaustively audit unrelated housekeeping tools to start local memory and drafts.
2. Show your human a short risk summary and propose one reviewed batch of relevant defaults. For stage 1, keep unchosen connectors/capabilities off where a reviewed narrow control is available; otherwise mark them deferred/unverified and unavailable for XO use, with unresolved exposure named, not disconnected or passed. No unchosen sign-in or route certification is required. Every relevant exposed connected-account or outbound mutation route must be mechanically blocked or its capability left off, not merely set to ask. A tool that can mutate a selected source account is relevant even if labeled unchosen or shared; the selected route stays unavailable until its actual current-session mutation gate passes before any source read. Ordinary approved work inside `~/xo/work` is not this category. If you cannot establish that boundary for a specific route, leave it off and record that route as unavailable; do not loop through global plugin deny-list changes to certify every app feature. A change that affects unrelated sessions needs its own clear scope, reversal, and yes.
3. Record the batch approval verbatim with a timestamp in the ledger. Check the resulting catalog once if the change needs a new session to load; absence supports that session's off state, not a universal runtime-denial claim. Do not reopen solely to turn an honest `unverified` into `passed`, or extend the deny list to unrelated tools after a relevant route is off. Revisit a route when the user selects it, a new tool or grant appears, or its effective setting changes.

Prefer the narrow session/project block or off control for a specific unused route; show scope and obtain approval before any change. Once its off state is established, stop—do not revoke account tokens, disconnect unrelated accounts, uninstall plugins or keep expanding deny lists to reinforce that result. If the control is unavailable or its result unknown, mark that route unavailable for XO use and state any unresolved exposure; do not claim it is off or safe. An off route needs no runtime denial test merely to proceed with chat work. If saved instructions keep demanding more cleanup, use `flows/repair-permission-loop.md`.

On Codex, this inventory answers only which connector tools are exposed in this session, not whether a selected account is reachable. Record account reach as unverified until a separately approved, gated account-specific check succeeds (`adapters/codex-app.md`, connector blocker).
